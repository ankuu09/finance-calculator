How the SIP Calculator Works

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) involves depositing a fixed sum of money into mutual funds at regular monthly intervals. This strategy enables rupee-cost averaging and harnesses compound interest.

FV = P × [((1 + i)n - 1) / i] × (1 + i)
  • FV: Future Value (Maturity Amount)
  • P: Amount invested regularly each month
  • i: Periodic rate of interest per month (Annual Rate / 12 / 100)
  • n: Total number of monthly installments (Years × 12)

Practical Example: If you invest ₹5,000 every month for 10 years at an expected annual return of 12%, your total capital contribution will be ₹6,00,000. Compounded monthly, your projected maturity wealth will be approximately ₹11,61,695, representing ₹5,61,695 in compounding returns.

SIP Calculator FAQs

Rupee cost averaging allows you to buy more mutual fund units when market prices (NAV) are low, and fewer units when prices are high. Over time, this smooths out market volatility without requiring you to time the market.
Yes, most asset management companies offer "Step-Up" or "Top-Up" SIP facilities where you can automatically increase your monthly investment by a percentage or fixed amount each year.

How the EMI Formula Works

An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) represents a fixed monthly amount payable to your lender. Each payment covers both interest and a portion of the principal.

EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)n] / [(1 + r)n - 1]
  • P: Principal loan amount borrowed
  • r: Monthly interest rate (Annual rate / 12 / 100)
  • n: Number of monthly installments (Years × 12)

EMI Calculator FAQs

Interest is calculated on the remaining unpaid principal balance. In the beginning, the balance is at its highest, meaning a larger portion of each EMI goes toward paying interest. Over time, as principal is reduced, the interest portion shrinks.
A longer loan tenure lowers your monthly EMI payment, making it easier on your monthly budget. However, a longer tenure substantially increases the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

How Fixed Deposit Interest is Calculated

Most retail banks compound interest on Fixed Deposits on a quarterly basis (4 times per year). The compounded formula is:

A = P × (1 + r / n)(n × t)
  • A: Total maturity amount
  • P: Initial principal amount deposited
  • r: Annual nominal interest rate (as a decimal)
  • n: Number of compounding periods per year (Quarterly = 4)
  • t: Number of years for which deposit is held

Fixed Deposit FAQs

The Mechanics of Compound Interest

Unlike simple interest which pays returns solely on the initial deposit, compound interest earns returns on both your principal and the accumulated interest from prior periods.

A = P × (1 + r / n)nt + PMT × [((1 + r / n)nt - 1) / (r / n)]

Over long time horizons, compounding creates an exponential curve where earnings from interest eventually surpass the capital deposited.

Compound Interest FAQs

The Rule of 72 is a quick mental math shortcut to estimate how many years it will take to double your money. Divide 72 by your annual rate of return (e.g., at 12%, 72 / 12 = 6 years to double).
The more frequently interest is compounded (e.g., daily vs. monthly vs. annually), the faster interest begins earning interest of its own, resulting in a slightly higher effective annual yield.

How GST is Computed

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is computed depending on whether your sticker price includes tax or requires adding tax:

GST Exclusive (Add Tax):
GST Amount = (Base Amount × GST Rate) / 100
Gross Total = Base Amount + GST Amount

GST Inclusive (Extract Tax):
Pre-GST Base = Gross Total / (1 + (GST Rate / 100))
GST Amount = Gross Total - Pre-GST Base

For intrastate transactions, the calculated GST is split equally between CGST (Central Government) and SGST (State Government).

GST Calculator FAQs

When a sale happens within the same state (intrastate), CGST and SGST are collected equally. When a sale occurs across state borders (interstate), Integrated GST (IGST) is collected by the Central Government.
In an inclusive GST invoice (such as retail consumer goods with MRP), the listed price already contains the tax component. Our calculator separates the true base price from the embedded tax.

Why Prepayments Make a Big Difference

When you add an extra payment each month, that additional amount goes directly toward reducing the loan principal balance. Since interest in subsequent months is charged on a smaller principal, your total debt compound slows down drastically.

Loan Prepayment FAQs

In most countries (including India for floating rate personal home loans per RBI guidelines), banks are prohibited from charging prepayment penalties. However, fixed-rate loans or commercial loans may carry prepayment terms. Check your loan sanction letter.
Shortening your tenure saves significantly more interest over the long run. Keeping the tenure long and reducing EMI provides more monthly cash flow flexibility, but yields lower total interest savings.
