How the Fixed Deposit Formula Works

Fixed deposits in commercial banking use the compound interest formula with quarterly or period compounding:

A = P × (1 + r / n)(n × t)
  • A: Total maturity amount at expiration
  • P: Principal deposit amount
  • r: Annual nominal interest rate (in decimal, e.g., 7% = 0.07)
  • n: Number of compounding periods per year (Quarterly = 4)
  • t: Total tenure duration in years

Practical Example of Fixed Deposit Returns

Assume an investor deposits ₹5,00,000 in a 5-year fixed deposit at an annual interest rate of 7.1% compounded quarterly:

  • Principal (P): ₹5,00,000
  • Annual rate (r): 0.071
  • Compounding frequency (n): 4 times per year
  • Tenure in years (t): 5 years (total 20 compounding quarters)
  • Total maturity value (A): ₹7,10,341
  • Total interest income: ₹7,10,341 - ₹5,00,000 = ₹2,10,341
Important Financial Estimation Notice Actual maturity values from banks may vary slightly due to daily leap-year day count conventions (365 vs 366 days), premature withdrawal penalties, and applicable Tax Deducted at Source (TDS). Check specific terms with your financial institution.
