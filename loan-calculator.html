How Loan Amortization and Prepayments Work

Standard reducing-balance amortization divides every regular payment into two components:

Monthly Interest = Unpaid Principal Balance × (Annual Rate / 12 / 100)
Principal Repayment = Total Monthly Payment - Monthly Interest

When you make an extra prepayment, 100% of that additional money bypasses interest and attacks the principal balance directly. Because future interest is calculated exclusively on remaining principal, every extra prepayment permanently reduces future interest charges for the remaining duration of the loan.

Practical Example of Loan Prepayment Impact

Consider a loan of ₹15,00,000 at 9% interest over a 15-year tenure:

  • Standard base monthly EMI: ₹15,214
  • Standard total interest over 15 years: ₹12,38,506
  • Now suppose you pay an extra ₹2,500/month (total ₹17,714/month):
  • Your loan is paid off in approximately 11.5 years instead of 15 years (saving 3.5 years of debt!).
  • Total interest drops to approximately ₹9,12,000, putting ₹3,26,000+ back in your pocket.
Important Financial Estimation Notice Actual prepayment credits depend on how your lender applies prepayments (whether immediately on the date received or on the next cycle billing date). Some institutions require written notice to allocate prepayments toward principal reduction. Contact your loan officer to confirm your bank's specific prepayment policy.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Every additional dollar or rupee paid above the required EMI is credited directly toward reducing the principal balance. With a lower principal, future monthly interest drops, creating a compounding savings cycle.
Many regulatory authorities (like RBI in India or CFPB in the USA) prohibit prepayment penalties on floating-rate home loans. However, fixed-rate loans or personal loans may carry prepayment charges. Review your loan agreement terms.
If your expected after-tax investment return exceeds your loan's interest rate, investing may generate higher net worth. However, loan prepayment provides a risk-free guaranteed return equal to the loan interest rate.
